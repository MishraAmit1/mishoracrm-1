# SaaS CRM — Sales & Reseller Guide

**Audience:** Anyone selling this platform to a prospective business — resellers, sales staff, partners.

Use this to understand what you're selling, who to pitch it to, and how to answer the questions prospects actually ask.

---

## 1. The 30-Second Pitch

> "One CRM that adapts to your business — whether you sell products, manufacture goods, or run a service business, it's the same platform, and you only pay for the pieces you actually use."

Most CRMs are built for one kind of business and force everyone else to make do. This platform ships **three business models in one codebase** — a general sales CRM core, a full Manufacturing/Purchasing suite, and a complete Service-Business suite (subscriptions, bookings, time billing, helpdesk) — switched on or off per customer through their plan. A gym, an IT AMC company, a coaching institute, and a small manufacturer can all run on the same platform without any of them seeing features irrelevant to them.

The common thread across every service-business prospect: **they're currently doing subscription tracking, booking, or support in spreadsheets/WhatsApp/paper — and losing renewals, no-shows, and complaints to that gap.**

## 3. Feature Highlights (by category)

**Sales & CRM Core** — Leads (with automatic capture from Facebook/Instagram Lead Ads, IndiaMART, JustDial, TradeIndia, Sulekha), Contacts, Deal pipeline with kanban/funnel analytics, Tasks & Follow-ups, Calendar, Quotations with a **customer-facing e-sign link (no login required)**, Invoicing with partial-payment tracking and branded PDFs, duplicate detection & merge.

**Manufacturing & Purchasing** — Product catalog with low-stock alerts, vendor quote comparison, the full Purchase Request → Approval → Purchase Order → Receive → Stock workflow, Work Orders with per-job cost tracking (labor/machine/material), batch/lot traceability.

**Service Business Suite** — Service catalog with bundleable packages, customer Subscription tracking with **fully customizable automatic Email/WhatsApp expiry reminders**, **public online Appointment booking** (shareable link, configurable business hours & capacity, automatic customer reminders and automatic no-show detection), Time Tracking that converts straight into invoices, a Ticket/Helpdesk system with a **public submission form, auto-generated ticket numbers, automatic confirmation messages, and SLA breach alerts** so nothing falls through the cracks.

**Communication** — Email and WhatsApp (send, bulk-send, templates, full logs; bring-your-own SMTP), a WhatsApp chatbot flow builder, Instagram comment/DM automation, Slack alerts for the team.

**Automation & Reliability** — SLA breach detection (leads and tickets), automatic reminders (follow-ups, tasks, invoices, subscriptions, appointments), automatic no-show handling — the platform proactively surfaces what needs attention instead of making staff remember to check.

**Reports & Insights** — Dashboards and dedicated report tabs for Overview, Deals, Revenue, Staff performance, and — when active — Subscriptions, Appointments, Tickets, and Time Tracking.

**Access Control & Security** — Role-based permissions out of the box (Admin/Staff), fully customizable roles for anything more granular, per-module access control, audit logs.

**Integrations** — A versioned REST API with tenant-issued API keys, outgoing webhooks for connecting to tools like Zapier/n8n, and (as a white-glove option) a request-based custom automation service — the platform team builds bespoke workflow automations on request.

## 4. How Plans & Pricing Work

Every customer subscribes to a **Plan**. Each plan defines:
- Hard limits (max leads, max team members — or unlimited).
- Which optional modules are switched on: WhatsApp, Advanced Reports, Social Media Leads, Manufacturing, Service Catalog, Subscriptions, Appointments/Booking, Time Tracking, Tickets/Helpdesk.
- Monthly or yearly pricing (with an automatic discount for annual commitment).

**This means you can position plans by vertical, not just by size:**
- A "Starter" plan with just the CRM core suits anyone getting started.
- A "Service Pro" plan adds Subscriptions + Appointments + Time Tracking + Tickets for service businesses.
- A "Manufacturing" plan adds the Purchasing/Work Order suite.
- Individual customers can also get one-off module overrides from the platform side if they need something outside their plan temporarily (e.g. a trial of Tickets before upgrading) — a flexible upsell lever.

Coupons/discount codes are also supported for promotions.

## 6. Objection Handling

- **"We already use WhatsApp/Excel for this."** — That's exactly the gap: nothing reminds itself, nothing tracks itself, and nothing survives someone leaving the team. This platform's whole value is doing the remembering and reminding automatically.
- **"Is it only for [gym / manufacturing / IT]?"** — No — it's one platform, and you only turn on (and pay for) the modules relevant to your business. A trading company, a service business, and a mixed business (sells products *and* offers services) can all run on it.
- **"Can our customers use it without installing anything?"** — Yes. Quotation approval, appointment booking, and support ticket submission are all link-based, no login, no app — this is a deliberate design choice, not a limitation.
- **"What if we need something custom?"** — Two paths: (1) custom fields and custom roles are self-service, no developer needed; (2) for a bespoke automated workflow, there's a request-based automation service where the platform team builds it for you.
- **"Can we control who on our team sees what?"** — Yes, granular role-based permissions, fully customizable per tenant.

## 7. What NOT To Oversell

Be accurate — a prospect who's disappointed after signing is worse than a slower close:

- "AI Automation" in the product is a **request form for a custom build**, not a drag-and-drop automation builder they can use themselves the moment they sign up.
- There's no customer login portal (yet) — customer-facing pages are link-based per record (one quotation, one appointment, one ticket), not a single dashboard where a customer logs in to see everything.
- Subscription renewal still requires a staff member to click "Renew" after the reminder goes out — it is not (yet) a fully automatic recurring billing engine for the tenant's *own* customers.
- Appointments are capacity-based (X people per slot), not "book a specific staff member" — if a prospect specifically needs per-technician scheduling, flag it rather than promise it.

---

## 8. Closing Checklist

1. Identify the prospect's business type → map to the relevant feature set above.
2. Identify their current pain (spreadsheets, missed renewals, lost complaints, no visibility into stock) → tie it directly to the automation that solves it.
3. Recommend a plan tier that matches which modules they actually need — don't oversell modules they won't use.
4. Offer a walkthrough of the one or two features that matter most to them, not a tour of everything.
5. Set expectations correctly using §7 above.
